02

The problem

Lab automation usually describes chemistry with discrete steps: add A to B, stir, heat, wait. A lot of real lab behavior can't be captured that way. The outcome depends on continuous, physical details those steps leave out: how fast the liquid goes in, at what angle, how deep the tip sits, how the solution is stirred.

Our case: an accidental discovery

Adding polysulfide (Sn2−) to mercury-containing wastewater (Hg2+) produces visibly different products depending on the stirring profile and how fast the liquid is added.

Why it matters. The yellow HgS has poor crystallinity. It can release Hg2+ back into the water, where it poisons people. Whether a mercury cleanup actually works depends on motion details that nobody records.

It's not just mercury

The same kind of problem shows up across bio and chem labs:

  • Cell culture
  • Drug discovery
  • Materials discovery

Our approach

We're building our own 3-DOF robot arm that carries an electric pipette. It explores the physical parameters of a reaction to find which motions change crystallinity.

Data

About 3 hours of high-quality teleoperated demonstrations on the HgS reaction. Each episode records:

  • Operation data: position, velocity and motor current for Z, Rz and Ry over time
  • Dispensing data: volume and rate from the electric pipette
  • Video of the vessel throughout the reaction
  • Outcome: which product formed, and its crystallinity

04

The outcome

What success looks like with a PI model in the loop.

1

The PI model learns to run our hardware

Trained on our teleoperation data and video, it controls the arm and the electric pipette directly.

2

It makes both crystals, with no prior knowledge

Without any built-in chemistry knowledge, the model learns to produce either product on demand: the well-crystallized HgS or the poorly crystalline yellow one.

3

It explains the difference

The model summarizes which operational differences in depth, stirring, dispense rate and tilt lead to each crystal.

Hypothetical example of a summary yellow product ← fast dispense, shallow tip, low stir
dark product   ← slow dispense, deep tip, steady high stir

05

Why it matters to Physical Intelligence

This project tests two things that are hard to show with standard robot arms and standard lab tasks.

Generalization

Runs on hardware nobody planned for

A 3-DOF arm carrying a pipette is nothing like the arms PI models usually run on. Getting a PI policy working on it shows the models carry over to the custom, one-off hardware that real labs build.

New kind of application

From lab assistant to discovery tool

Most robotics-for-science work replaces simple operator jobs like shaking tubes and moving plates. Here the PI model produces new scientific data in chemistry and biology, where the motion itself is the experimental variable.
